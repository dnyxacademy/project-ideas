# Project ideas

You build **one project across all four weeks** of the Dnyx Academy training-cum-internship. It
grows every week, and by 28 November it is something you can show an employer. This repository is
where you choose it.

**Pick in Week 1. Get it approved by Tuesday.** Then do not change it — restarting in Week 3 is the
most reliable way to not finish.

## How to choose

1. **Will you still care about it in Week 3?** That is the only question that matters. Week 3 is the
   heaviest week, and interest is what gets you through it.
2. **Be honest about your level.** "Gentle" is not a lesser choice. A finished small project scores
   far better than an ambitious half-built one, and mentors have seen plenty of both.
3. **Check you can get the data** before you commit, if you are on the AI track.
4. **Do not pick what your friend picked** just to compare notes. You will both learn less, and
   identical repositories raise questions we would rather not have to ask.

### Levels

| Level | Means |
|---|---|
| gentle | You have not written code before. The minimum is reachable by Week 3, leaving room to polish |
| comfortable | You can give the four weeks properly. Most people should be here |
| ambitious | You have built something before. There is real difficulty in it, and it can be cut back if it slips |

## What every project must have by 28 November

Each idea page lists its own minimum. On top of that, your track has a bar that the final project is
marked against — it is in your starter repository, in `docs/week-plan.md`:

- **Web:** sign-in, three related PostgreSQL tables, a Next.js front end in TypeScript, validation,
  eight automated tests including one end-to-end flow, deployed front end and API, a README, a
  two-minute video, and a "How I used AI" note.
- **AI:** your Weeks 1 to 3 work still visible, an agent with three tools (one through MCP), logged
  traces, ten test cases with results and an honest failure analysis, a deployed demo, a README with
  limitations, a video, and a "How I used AI" note.

## Scope, honestly

Every idea here is deliberately smaller than the project catalogues you will find online. Those
describe platforms built over months by people who already know the stack. You have four weeks, at
three to four hours a day, starting from the first lesson.

So the minimum on each page is genuinely minimal, and the stretch goals are there for when you are
ahead — not a checklist. **Shipping the minimum, tested and deployed, with an honest README, is a
pass and then some.** A sprawling unfinished platform is not.

## More ideas, and places to go deeper

Other people's lists, which are worth browsing — especially if none of the briefs above appeals.
They are not ours, we do not control them, and most are pitched at a bigger scope than four weeks.
Read them for the idea, then cut it down to the minimum you can actually ship. Mentors will help you
decide what to cut.

### Idea catalogues

| Where | What you get |
|---|---|
| [NamasteDev project ideas](https://namastedev.com/project-ideas) | Product-style briefs with a difficulty and a stack, mostly platform-sized |
| [roadmap.sh projects](https://roadmap.sh/projects) | Ideas graded beginner to advanced, with requirements per project |
| [Frontend Mentor challenges](https://www.frontendmentor.io/challenges) | Real designs to build to. Excellent for Weeks 1 and 2 practice |
| [Project-based learning](https://github.com/practical-tutorials/project-based-learning) | "Build X from scratch" tutorials, by language |
| [The Odin Project](https://www.theodinproject.com/paths) | A full project-first curriculum. Our own plan borrows its SQL-before-NoSQL order |

### Data and documents, for the AI track

| Where | What you get |
|---|---|
| [Kaggle datasets](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets) | The widest choice, with notebooks showing what others did |
| [Hugging Face datasets](https://huggingface.co/datasets) | Text and multimodal sets, easy to load in a notebook |
| [UCI repository](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/datasets) | Small, clean, well-documented teaching sets |
| [data.gov.in](https://data.gov.in) | Indian government open data, by ministry and district |
| [Public APIs](https://github.com/public-apis/public-apis) | Free APIs — useful for an agent tool in Week 4 |

### Learning and reference

| Where | What for |
|---|---|
| [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Learn_web_development) | The reference for HTML, CSS and JavaScript. Trust it over a blog post |
| [Hugging Face agents course](https://huggingface.co/learn/agents-course/unit0/introduction) | Free course on agents, which is where Week 4 goes |
| [Model Context Protocol docs](https://modelcontextprotocol.io) | MCP itself, for the Week 4 tool |

**Two cautions.** A tutorial you can follow line by line teaches you very little — read it, close
it, then build. And whatever you borrow from an idea list, the project has to be your own work: your
commits, your decisions, and your answers in the review.
